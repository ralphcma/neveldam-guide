Neveldam: A Player’s Field Guide

A guide to the investigation, without giving away the answers.

Holmes has asked you to look into a death in Neveldam. The accounts don’t quite add up, and someone is trying to steer you in the wrong direction. Here’s some help getting started, following your leads and keeping track of the evidence.

1. Your first five minutes

Choose your difficulty before you go in

If you don’t already have a case open, entering 221B starts one automatically. Choose your difficulty at The Baker Street Doorstep first. Once a case is open, its difficulty stays the same until you finish or close it.

  1. From Neveldam Shuttle Landing, go east to The Baker Street Doorstep.
  2. Pick a difficulty: DIFFICULTY ASSISTED, DIFFICULTY DETECTIVE or DIFFICULTY EXPERT. Detective is the default. If you’d like some extra help on your first visit, try Assisted.
  3. Type ENTER 221B and read Holmes’s introduction. Then use READ NOTEBOOK to see where to begin.
  4. Use LEADS NOTEBOOK for your first leads. From the Sitting Room, go east to The Consulting Table, then east again to The Identification Archive. READ CARDS lists the people in the case; EXAMINE <surname> opens a person’s dossier.
  5. Choose either the visitor’s or the victim’s trail. Visit the person named in your notebook and read the papers they direct you to. Check that notebook again whenever you need your next lead.

ENTER 221B

Send one command at a time. In examples such as <surname> or <paper>, use the name shown in your case and leave out the angle brackets.

If you’ve just closed a case in the Sitting Room, choose your next difficulty there and type BEGIN DOCKET. You don’t need to leave and re-enter.

2. Choosing a difficulty

ModeWhat changes
AssistedSome of the paperwork has already been checked and countersigned for you. You can also use HINTS NOTEBOOK when you want help comparing the evidence.
DetectiveThe default. Follow the paper trails and collect independent witness observations to work out who the accounts describe.
ExpertMore people share the same identifying features, so you may need extra observations to tell them apart. Witnesses follow their usual rounds.

Check your current difficulty with DIFFICULTY NOTEBOOK. Meal prices, witness speed and the final verdict rules are the same in every mode. To change difficulty, finish the current case or close it and start another. Closing an unfinished case discards its findings.

What the colours mean

The colours draw your attention to names and useful details. They don’t tell you which account is true. Read the whole passage, including anything the witness couldn’t see or the record leaves uncertain.

4. Following the evidence

As you investigate, ask yourself: what am I being told, what does the original record actually say, and is there another source that backs it up—or contradicts it? A convincing story still needs checking.

People

  • Start with the dossiers at The Identification Archive. Several people may share a feature, so look for someone who fits all the details you’ve gathered.
  • Keep track of which person you’re investigating. PURSUIT NOTEBOOK follows the visitor; IDENTITY NOTEBOOK follows the victim.
  • FOLLOW <surname> shows someone your reference; it doesn’t move you to their location. Go to the office they name, read the original with READ <paper>, then check your notebook for the next step.
  • OBSERVATIONS NOTEBOOK lets you reread the signed observations you’ve collected. An empty notebook just means you haven’t collected any yet.

Place

  • Follow the leads in SETTINGS NOTEBOOK and compare the descriptions the witnesses left in their separate records.
  • When you visit a possible room, use EXAMINE FURNISHINGS to record how it’s arranged. ROOMNOTES NOTEBOOK lets you look back over the observations you’ve received.
  • Remember that a paper can describe somewhere else. The office holding it isn’t necessarily where the meeting happened.

Death and weapon

  • CLINICAL NOTEBOOK lists seven doctors’ reports and tells you where each is kept. Go to the named office and use EXAMINE <surname>. These are medical reports, not the personal dossiers you read at The Identification Archive.
  • At the comparison laboratory, EXAMINE EXHIBITS lists the drawers. Examine each one using the name shown, then use READ CATALOGUE whenever you want to look back over the exhibits.
  • Before you can read the sealed specimen report, you’ll need all seven medical reports, all 24 exhibit examinations, the room observations and the specimen’s custody trail. MATERIAL NOTEBOOK, DIRECTORY NOTEBOOK and REFERRALS NOTEBOOK will help you follow the paperwork.
  • Once that’s done, return to the laboratory and type EXAMINE SPECIMEN. Compare its material signature and recorded detail with the catalogue. The method of death and the particular object used are two separate answers.

Motive

  • Start with PAPERS NOTEBOOK. Use PAPERS <surname> to show a reference to the person holding it, then READ <copy-name> at the desk they send you to.
  • LETTERS NOTEBOOK tells you where the original letters are kept. Examine them using their displayed names. PAPERNOTES NOTEBOOK lets you reread what you’ve learned about the torn fragment.
  • Look at the physical details of the paper as well as what the letter says. A dispute in the files isn’t necessarily the motive for this death.

Deception

  • These accounts offer different explanations of how someone misled the investigation. You’ll need to test them. INQUIRIES NOTEBOOK explains which details each explanation relies on.
  • Use COMPARE <surname> to ask the named person to check your references. Then use READ Signed Enclosure at the office they name to read the signed comparison.
  • RECORDS NOTEBOOK lists the original records and where to find them. Examine each by its wrapper’s displayed name. Look back at your comparisons with FINDINGS NOTEBOOK, and your examined originals with RECORDNOTES NOTEBOOK.
  • After examining the originals and gathering enough comparisons, go to The Casebook Gallery and use COMPARE COUNTERFOIL for the final signed comparison. You don’t have to read every reference to get there.
Do you have to read everything?

Not always. You may identify a person or rule out an account before following every available lead. The specimen report does have a checklist, though: you’ll need the medical reports, exhibits, room observations and custody trail described above.

To come back, use RETURN CARRIAGE at the district’s arrival office. You can investigate all four districts, regardless of the filing tabs on your papers.

Witnesses move around. Check WITNESSES NOTEBOOK for their rounds and meeting places. At the tavern listed for that witness, use ARRANGE <surname> to leave a message, then WAIT <surname> until they arrive. Use FOLLOW <surname> to discuss your reference once they’re there. TALK <surname> starts an ordinary conversation; it doesn’t present the reference.

You can log out and come back without losing the case. Its records stay in the same places, even if you leave 221B. When you return, check your notebooks to pick up where you left off—there’s no need to reset. Keep an eye on your stamina, and leave yourself enough money for meals.

6. Keeping useful notes

You don’t need to copy every description. A few short notes can make it much easier to see what you know and what you still need to check. For each useful observation, jot down:

Where it came fromWhat it tells youWhat it leaves openWhat to check next
The witness or record, and where you found itThe detail you can actually point toAnything uncertain, missing or outside the source’s knowledgeAnother source that could confirm or challenge it

Keep separate notes for the visitor, victim, place, method, weapon, motive and deception. With times, note what happened and which clock or record the time came from. It’s fine to leave a question unanswered for now. Mark guesses as guesses so they don’t get mixed in with the facts.

7. Present your case only when you are ready

Finish investigating before you type PRESENT CASE

Type PRESENT CASE at The Consulting Table when you’re ready to enter Holmes’s Private Study. Once inside, you can’t go back out for more evidence. Check ANSWERS NOTEBOOK and finish your investigation before going in.

In the study, you’ll enter eight conclusions in the ledger. The commands below show the format; fill them in using the names and terms from your own case.

ConclusionEntry command
The murdererSUSPECT <surname>
The victimVICTIM <surname>
The method of deathMETHOD <term>
The placeSCENE <place-key>
The motiveMOTIVE <term>
The deceptionTHEORY <term>
The supporting record (corroborating evidence)EVIDENCE <record-key>
The particular weapon or objectWEAPON <catalogue-key>

The evidence entry is for the record or wrapper you examined, not the weapon or a type of material. ANSWERS NOTEBOOK explains how to enter it.

Check your entries with READ LEDGER and REVIEW LEDGER. An entry being accepted doesn’t mean the answer is correct. To change one, type its command again with your new answer.

SUBMIT LEDGER ends the case

Check all eight entries before submitting. SUBMIT LEDGER ends the case and sends you back to the doorstep, even if your answers are wrong or incomplete. If you want a different difficulty next time, choose it before going back into 221B.

8. Closing a case and starting another

If you want to abandon the current case, go to 221B – The Sitting Room. RESTART NOTEBOOK explains the process in-game. To close the case, send these two commands separately:

Close your current case
RESET DOCKET
CONFIRM DOCKET
You’ll lose this case’s findings when you confirm

RESET DOCKET asks whether you want to close the case. CONFIRM DOCKET closes it and discards its findings. If you change your mind, use CANCEL DOCKET instead. Closing a case won’t start a new one for you.

To start again on Assisted
DIFFICULTY ASSISTED
BEGIN DOCKET

You can replace the Assisted line with DIFFICULTY DETECTIVE or DIFFICULTY EXPERT. Closing a case doesn’t remove completion records or rewards you’ve already earned, but it does discard that case’s evidence and findings. If you’re only taking a break or reconnecting, leave it open.

9. If you’re stuck

What’s happening?What to try
You can’t change difficultyYour case is already open. Finish it, or follow the reset instructions above to start over. Closing it will discard its findings.
Your identification observations are emptyRead PURSUIT NOTEBOOK or IDENTITY NOTEBOOK, follow a reference and read the original at the office it names.
You’re not getting a new leadCheck your notebook, the person’s name, the paper’s exact name and where it’s kept. Showing someone a reference and reading the paper they send you to are two separate steps.
You can’t find a witnessLook up their meeting place in WITNESSES NOTEBOOK, then arrange a meeting and wait at the right tavern. FOLLOW doesn’t move you there.
You can’t read the specimen reportCheck EVIDENCE NOTEBOOK: have you read all seven medical reports, examined all 24 exhibits, collected the room observations and finished the material custody trail?
A paper isn’t where you remember itCheck the notebook for this case. Papers can be assigned to different offices in a new case, so an old location list—or another player’s—may send you to the wrong place.
You’re ready to enter the study, except for one questionAnswer it before using PRESENT CASE. You won’t be able to go back out for more evidence, and SUBMIT LEDGER will end the case.

If you’d like a nudge, try the separate Optional Hints guide. The hints get more specific as you go, but won’t name the culprit or walk you through a solution. In Assisted mode, you can also ask for help with HINTS NOTEBOOK.
